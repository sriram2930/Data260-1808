4. Retroactive Grade Forgiveness
Purpose:
To request Grade Forgiveness (GF) for a repeated course when it was not automatically
applied.
What is Grade Forgiveness?
Grade Forgiveness (GF) lets you replace the grade from your first attempt in a course
with the grade from your second attempt:
The first grade still shows on your transcript, but it is removed from your GPA calculation
and units earned (UE).
This can help improve your GPA if you repeat a course.
Only the first and second attempts at a course count for GF.
Attempts after that are grade averaged.
GF only applies to SJSU courses (not transfer courses).
You may repeat a course for GF only once.
Condition
:
Only courses where the first grade was C- or lower qualify.
You can use GF for up to 16 units total (University Policy
F08-2 [pdf]
).
Special rules apply if your repeats happened before Fall 2009 (9 or 18 units max,
depending on when you transferred).
If you’ve already used up your 16 units but need to repeat a course to graduate, you
can petition for an exception. (Approved repeats will be grade-averaged, not forgiven.)
Special Topics courses or cross-listed courses required a memo from the department
stating courses are the same or have similar content. Attached memo to the petition.
Discontinued
course: a memo from the department stating course will longer be offered needs to
be attached to the petition. A similar course (GE Area or content) may be use in lieu
of the discontinue course.
Course content from the first attempt is included as part of a more comprehensive
course taken from the second attempt.
A first-year experience (FYE) GE course was repeated with a different GE course in
the same area. (University Policy
S09-4 [pdf]
).
Form:
Fill out the
Retroactive Grade Forgiveness Petition [DocuSign]
Quick Reminders:
✔ Both attempts must be taken at SJSU.
✔ Your academic standing for the semester of the second attempt must be posted (check
your unofficial transcript).
✔ “No Credit (NC)” grades and courses that are repeatable for credit do not qualify
(except during the pandemic terms Fall 2020 & Spring 2021, when temporary rules applied).

(Formerly
Excess Open University Units Petition
prior to Fall 2015)
SJSU accepts 24 units of Open University (OU) or baccalaureate extension courses.
While all grades count and requirements may be satisfied with units beyond this limit,
only this stated maximum can be used toward the 120 unit needed for a baccalaureate
degree—unless a petition is submitted and approved for the student.
Purpose:
Allow undergraduate students who have applied for graduation to request that OU units
(beyond the 24 unit limit accepted by SJSU) be applied towards a baccalaureate degree.
Eligibility Requirements:
For undergraduate students who have been matriculated (formally admitted and enrolled)
at SJSU for at least one semester; and have

Students must also demonstrate that excess OU units were accumulated as a result of
being
disqualified
from SJSU and becoming eligible for reinstatement, OR, that there were

beyond the student's control.
Consult with an advisor and/or Graduation Evaluator before submitting this request.
Students nearing 24 units of OU coursework should first contact an advisor. Students
who have reached 24 units and have a hold will need to contact an Advisor to remove

21. Healthcare Provider Verification of Medical Condition Form
Purpose:
This form is used to obtain professional verification from a healthcare provider when
a student requests to drop a course, withdraw from a semester, or take a leave of
absence for health-related reasons, including caring for a patient. Its purpose is
to confirm whether the student’s medical or mental health condition, in the provider’s
judgment,
is or was likely to significantly impact their ability to perform academically
.
The form does not require disclosure of specific medical details—only a professional
assessment of the condition’s impact on coursework. It also guides providers to distinguish
between poor academic performance due to medical reasons versus non-medical stresses,
and to identify whether certain types of coursework (e.g., physical activity, lab,
or fieldwork) are specifically affected.
The information on this form helps determine whether
accommodations
might allow course completion or if a
drop
or
withdrawal
is warranted.
Form
:
University’s Healthcare Provider Verification Form [pdf]
instead of any medical record.
